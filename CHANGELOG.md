# Changelog

All notable changes are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/) once releases start.

Every pull request that changes something a user or operator would notice adds a line under **Unreleased**.

## [Unreleased]

### Changed

- `ORPHUSBOT_NODE_TOKEN` is gone: enrol each node once with `orphusbot-node enrol <code>`. New node setting `ORPHUSBOT_NODE_KEY` (default `~/.config/orphusbot/node.json`).
- `ORPHUSBOT_UI_TOKEN` is replaced by `ORPHUSBOT_SETUP_TOKEN`, which only works until the first passkey is added. New setting `ORPHUSBOT_ORIGIN` (default `http://localhost:7480`). Open the page at `localhost`, not `127.0.0.1`.

### Added

- **Android app (M4.4):** the desktop app now also builds for Android. It's the same window onto your server: enter the server's address once, then sign in with **Sign in with another device** and approve the code from a browser where you have a passkey. Approvals, chat and everything else work as on the web. Build steps are in docs/DEVELOPMENT.md.
- **Ambient capture (M4.3):** turn on hands-free for a wearable (Captures, or the phone app) and what's said around you becomes suggestions: things you said you'd do, things asked of you, and what others owe you, each with the words it came from. Nothing becomes a task until you accept it, and nothing heard ever makes a bot reply or act. Every screen shows when listening is on, with a Pause button; the server can switch it off for everyone (`ORPHUSBOT_HANDS_FREE_ALLOWED=false`). No audio is kept, and the words are deleted after 7 days. Extraction is one call with no tools at all.
- **Usage (M4.2):** Settings → Usage shows what your bots' model calls used today and this week, by provider and model, and today by bot: turns, tokens and what it would have cost. Set `ORPHUSBOT_DAILY_TOKEN_BUDGET` and you get one warning on your wrist and phone when a day's use crosses it.
- **Push to your phone (M4.1):** set `ORPHUSBOT_NTFY_URL` to a topic on your own ntfy server and your phone gets a push when a bot needs you (an approval, a question, a takeover, or an ask your wrist didn't answer), when a task fails, and when work you didn't start from a chat finishes. Tapping one opens that bot. No Google account needed.
- **Decisions view (M3.6):** **Decisions** in the sidebar shows what System 1 decided and how it's doing: how often it was asked, abstained and agreed with you, and every receipt with its confidence, the threshold it needed and how long it took. Approving or denying a card it asked about, and changing the bot on a ticket it assigned, are kept as labels for calibrating it later.
- **The wearable talks to OrphusBot (M3.5):** pair it from Settings → Wearable (a one-time code for the app's pairing page). Tap, speak a task and tap again: it becomes a ticket, and the wrist asks "should I start?" unless System 1 is sure it's safe (tap starts it, double tap leaves it on the board). While it runs the wrist shows queued, running (breathing blue) and done (green flash, double buzz) or error. A bot's question or approval on a task you started from the wrist comes to the wrist one at a time; a tap can approve only a reversible file change you just started, and anything else is "seen" on the wrist and waits on your phone or desktop. Unanswered asks stop after 10 minutes. Settings shows every signal and how it was answered.
- **Tickets (M3.4):** a board (**Tickets** in the sidebar) with To do, Doing, Review, Blocked and Done, urgency and an assigned bot. Assigning a bot starts it on the ticket; when it finishes the ticket moves to Review (or Blocked if it failed). With System 1 on, new tickets get a bot and urgency filled in, and are started only when it's very sure that's safe. Mention `OB-12` in a message and the bot gets that ticket as data.
- **Routines (M3.3):** give a bot something to do on its own, from **Routines** in the sidebar: on a schedule (presets or cron, in your time zone), once at a set time, or whenever its webhook is called (the address is shown once; what's posted to it is given to the bot as data, not instructions). Each firing shows in the bot's thread. Routines skip, and say why, when the bot is disabled or busy, when they've used today's 20 runs, or (if you turn on pre-flight) when System 1 is sure there's nothing new. **Run now** fires one by hand.
- **System 1 with Laya (M3.2):** with laya-serve configured (`ORPHUSBOT_LAYA_URL`), messages to the Inbox go to the right bot when Laya is sure ("Sent to Researcher (System 1 was 92% sure)"), and file changes inside a bot's own computer can go ahead without a card when it's very sure. Shell commands and anything on your machine still always ask. Every decision is recorded and shown under **Decisions**. Without Laya nothing changes.
- **Bots (M3.1):** make as many bots as you like with **+ New bot**: a name, what it's for, instructions, an optional model and thinking level, and a private or team computer. Each bot has its own conversation, its own Orphus engine started with its settings, and its own computer. The **Inbox** bot always exists. Edit or disable a bot from the chat header; the sidebar shows each bot's latest message and what's waiting for you.
- **Several machines and placement (M2.3):** any number of nodes can be connected at once. A bot's work goes back to the machine holding its computer; otherwise to the least busy machine that may work. A laptop on battery, or a node in its quiet hours (`ORPHUSBOT_QUIET_HOURS=22-7`), takes no new work, and a waiting task says why. The chat header shows which machines are online and where the bot is working.
- **Bot screens and takeover (M2.2):** with the new desktop image (`images/desktop`), a bot's computer has a screen with Chromium. Press **Screen** in the chat to watch it live, and **Take control** to use it yourself. A bot can ask you to take over (for a login or a captcha); a card appears with **Open screen** and **Done**, and the bot carries on when you're finished. Screens go through the server and the node's existing link, so the computer opens no ports.
- **Bot computers (M2.1):** a bot's tools (read, write, edit, bash) now run in its own rootless Podman container, not on your machine. The container has no capabilities, a read-only system, its own home folder, resource limits, and internet access but no access to your LAN or NetBird addresses. Your model logins stay on the host. Set `ORPHUSBOT_RUNTIME=none` to keep the old behaviour.
- **Desktop app (M1.5):** `orphusbot-desktop`, a Tauri window onto your server's web UI. It stays on your server and opens other links in your browser; the first run asks for the server's address.
- **Sign in with another device (M1.5):** for the desktop app and any browser without passkeys. The device shows a code such as `ABCD-EFGH`; approve it from a signed-in browser (Settings → Link a device, or the link it shows) and the device gets its own sign-in, which ends if you remove the approving passkey.
- **New web UI (M1.4):** a Leptos app replaces the placeholder page. Bots are on the left (with a badge when one is waiting for you), the chat is on the right with approval and question cards and a Stop button, and Settings lists your passkeys and nodes so you can add or remove them and sign out. It works at phone width and in dark mode. Build it with `trunk build --release` in `crates/ui`; the server serves it from `ORPHUSBOT_UI_DIR`.
- **Node enrolment (M1.3):** each machine gets its own key instead of sharing a token. Choose **Add a node** on the page and run the command it shows (`orphusbot-node enrol <code>`) on the machine; the code works once, for 10 minutes. The node signs a fresh challenge every time it connects. Removing a node (`POST /api/nodes/remove`) disconnects it at once and keeps it out.
- **Passkey sign-in (M1.3):** sign in with a passkey instead of a shared token. On first run, the setup token signs you in for an hour so you can add a passkey; after that it stops working. Sessions last 30 days and only their hashes are stored. Removing a passkey signs out its sessions, and removing all of them turns the setup token back on. New guide for running the server over NetBird with HTTPS: [docs/DEPLOY.md](docs/DEPLOY.md). Nodes can connect over `wss://`.
- **Approvals and the effect ledger (M1.2):** every tool call the bot makes goes to OrphusBot first through the `orphusbot-bridge` Orphus extension, which the node loads automatically. Read-only tools run; anything else shows an approval card (Allow once, Always allow this tool, Deny) unless a rule already decides. A resumed run can't repeat a call that already completed: it gets the earlier result instead, and a call that may have run before it was interrupted is flagged for you. Questions a bot asks appear as cards you can answer.
- **Durable runs (M1.1):** each message becomes a run in Postgres. If the node drops or goes silent mid-run, the run goes back to the queue and resumes, with its context, when a node is available again; a late report from the interrupted attempt is ignored. A message sent while the bot is waiting joins its run, and one sent while it is working steers it. A **Stop** button cancels the run.
- **Milestone M0:** a Rust workspace with `orphusbot-proto`, `orphusbot-server` and `orphusbot-node`. A message sent from the browser is stored in Postgres, handed to an `orphus --mode rpc` engine on a node, and the reply streams back to every open browser over Server-Sent Events, resuming from the last event after a reconnect. See [docs/DEVELOPMENT.md](docs/DEVELOPMENT.md).
- Rust CI: formatting, Clippy with warnings as errors, tests against Postgres 18, and `cargo deny` for licences and advisories.
- The wearable (firmware, Flutter companion app, hardware, device-channel contract and docs) in `wearable/`, imported as a cleaned snapshot ([DECISIONS D-012](docs/DECISIONS.md)).
- `.gitleaksignore` listing the reviewed secret-scan false positives.
- Product requirements ([docs/PRD.md](docs/PRD.md)), design ([docs/DESIGN.md](docs/DESIGN.md)), decision log ([docs/DECISIONS.md](docs/DECISIONS.md)) and the Rakazo architecture study.
- Contribution rules: documentation-in-the-same-PR, the ponytail method and Rust practices ([CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md), [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md)).
- CodeRabbit review configuration and a CI check that pull requests update docs and the changelog.

### Fixed

- Choice lists (a bot's thinking level, a ticket's bot, urgency and status, a routine's bot and schedule) showed their first option instead of the saved one, so editing a bot could reset its thinking level. They now show the saved value.
- The ticket board now refreshes while it's open, so a bot System 1 assigns to a new ticket appears without reopening it.
