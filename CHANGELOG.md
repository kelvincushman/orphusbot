# Changelog

All notable changes are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/) once releases start.

Every pull request that changes something a user or operator would notice adds a line under **Unreleased**.

## [Unreleased]

### Changed

- Ideas Room **fast voice** (the default): a live, hands-free conversation with Grok's realtime voice, which answers in about a second, lets you talk over it, searches the web, and asks your Orchestrator for anything about your own work. The Orchestrator's own voice is a toggle. New settings `ORPHUSBOT_XAI_KEY`, `ORPHUSBOT_XAI_VOICE`.
- Ideas Room is much quicker: room turns use a fast model with thinking off, and the reply is spoken sentence by sentence while it's being written.
- Bots can ask to install a skill they wrote: you get a card with the whole skill and choose Allow or Deny, every time.
- **Ideas Room** (in the menu): brainstorm with the Orchestrator by voice. Tap the microphone and talk; your words are written down on the box (qwen-stt), the Orchestrator answers conversationally and can send Researcher to search the web, and the reply is spoken (ElevenLabs) while a colour orb listens, thinks and speaks with you. New settings `ORPHUSBOT_STT_URL`, `ORPHUSBOT_STT_TOKEN`, `ORPHUSBOT_ELEVENLABS_KEY`, `ORPHUSBOT_ELEVENLABS_VOICE`.
- Gallery: rename items and add or remove tags on a full-screen details page (tap an item, or long-press it when attaching from the gallery). On phones the old dialog hid these fields.
- The app has its own logo (a robot head with a coiled antenna and a cyan light) as its icon on Android, Mac and Linux, and an opening animation when it starts.
- Media: Nano Banana 2.1 (up to 4K) and Seedance 2.5 (4 to 30 s, 1080p, first and last frames) are the new defaults. Videos can be drafted cheaply at 480p for approval, then finished at 1080p.
- Bots' computers now include the Rive CLI. Media Creator makes animations in three steps: concept images, your approval, then the Rive file (`rive-animation` skill). Rebuild the desktop image (`podman build -t localhost/orphusbot-desktop images/desktop`).
- Bots can no longer pass a job back to the teammate who asked for it (the Orchestrator and PA loop); they answer, or say why they can't.
- New server setting `ORPHUSBOT_PUSH_HOSTS`: phones can only register for pushes through your own ntfy server, never a public one.
- The app has a new look, after Grok's app: black or white, the bot's face in a pill at the top, the menu behind a button (beside the chat on wide screens), replies as plain text, and a rounded composer with attach, model and send. The bots' own faces and robots stay.
- **Schedules** in the app (the clock icon): have a bot do something on its own every day, on weekdays, weekly, every few hours, once, by cron or by webhook, with Run now, pause and edit.
- Every copy of the app now knows its build. `scripts/publish-app.sh` publishes the newest Android build on the server at `/app`, and an older app shows **Update**. `scripts/macos-setup.sh` now builds the same Flutter app for the Mac, replacing the Tauri wrapper. New server setting `ORPHUSBOT_APP_DIR` (default `~/.local/share/orphusbot/app`).
- Social drafts must go through the [humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer) skill (installed on the node): the phone tool refuses a `draft` without `"humanized": true` and records the humanizer version on the review.
- Posted X items in Social's Pipeline link to your X profile ("See it on X"), and items to check link to it too ("Check your profile").
- Social's Calendar tab is now **Pipeline**: what needs checking, what's sending, what's waiting to send, and what's posted. Unconfirmed posts get "Yes, it posted" and "No, send it again", and you get a push when something posts or needs checking.
- New per-bot setting **Media generation**: the bot gets a `media` tool that makes images (Nano Banana 2, GPT Image 2) and video (Seedance 2) through kie.ai and saves them in its computer. New node file `~/.config/orphusbot/keys.env` for API keys (`ORPHUSBOT_KIE_KEY`); see `deploy/keys.env.example`.
- The phone tool asks for a commenter's exact @handle and exact comment text when drafting an X reply, so ContentSwarm can find the comment again and send the approved reply.
- `ORPHUSBOT_NODE_TOKEN` is gone: enrol each node once with `orphusbot-node enrol <code>`. New node setting `ORPHUSBOT_NODE_KEY` (default `~/.config/orphusbot/node.json`).
- `ORPHUSBOT_UI_TOKEN` is replaced by `ORPHUSBOT_SETUP_TOKEN`, which only works until the first passkey is added. New setting `ORPHUSBOT_ORIGIN` (default `http://localhost:7480`). Open the page at `localhost`, not `127.0.0.1`.

### Added

- **Phone and social bots:** a new bot setting, **Phone and social accounts**: none, read accounts and queue drafts, or also use the phone. Bots with it can read your accounts' voice, queue posts and replies for your approval, and (if allowed) look at and use your phone. Anything that could post, reply, like, follow or confirm on the phone asks you every time, even if you've said "always" before.
- **Social:** the megaphone in the header opens your X and LinkedIn drafts, shown the way they'll be posted, to approve, edit, schedule or reject (say why and the next drafts learn). Replies show under the comment they answer, the Calendar shows what's scheduled and what went out, the Phone tab shows the phone's screen live, and Voice holds each account's voice and what it knows. A notification tells you when new drafts arrive. Posting goes through your own phone, plugged into the server, and nothing goes out without your tap.
- **Omarchy computers:** give a bot an Omarchy desktop in its settings (Its computer's desktop → Omarchy). Its computer becomes Arch Linux with Omarchy's look, bar, terminal and keys, which you can watch and take over as before, and the bot can take screenshots, click and type on it itself. Its home folder carries over when you switch.
- **Teach System 1 in the app:** Settings → Decisions lists where kev sent messages and which tool calls it judged; mark each right or wrong, and Fit tunes how sure it must be from your answers.
- **Fixed:** the laptop app switched to the phone layout (no sidebar or message box) when its window was under 640 px wide; desktops now always keep the bot list beside the chat.
- **System 1 is on, running kev:** the decision model that routes jobs to the right bot and lets harmless edits inside a bot's computer through without asking now runs on Kev-9B on the GPU box. Anything irreversible still always asks you.
- **Robots with bodies:** every bot is now a whole robot on legs, wheels, tracks, rotors, a pogo spring, a jet or snake segments; poke it and it reacts, leave it idle and it fidgets, and when a job fails it blows itself up, then sits there dizzy until the next go.
- **Files open in the app:** tap anything a bot made to read PDFs page by page, watch video, play audio, zoom pictures, and read Markdown or code nicely formatted; PDFs and documents show a preview right in the chat. Bots are told to share documents as Markdown or PDF with clear names.
- **Phone notifications:** the Android app gets a notification when a bot needs you, finishes or fails, through the ntfy app and your own ntfy server (no Google); tapping it opens that bot.
- **The app on Android (M12.1):** the Flutter app now builds for Android phones and connects to your server's https address; on a phone the bot list and each conversation get the full screen, and back returns to the list.
- **A library of bot faces (M11.2):** choose any of about 30 animated faces for any bot (Settings → the bot → Face); every face shows the same five moods, and the bar panel and discussions use it too.
- **Token usage per bot:** each bot's chat shows its tokens today and this week, and Settings → Usage lists every bot's tokens and cost.
- **The Gallery (M11.1):** a place to keep pictures, video, audio and documents for your bots. Add files (up to 2 GB each), tag and describe them, mark which bots should know about them, and pick them straight into a chat with 📎 → From the gallery. Files a bot makes can be saved to the gallery with one click.
- **Bots know what their teammates can do (M10.2):** each bot now knows every teammate's model, Google access and connections, and its own limits, so it hands a job to the bot that can actually do it (for example, asks PA to add a calendar event) instead of trying and failing.
- **Google for your bots (M10.1):** bots can look things up in your Gmail, Calendar, Tasks, Drive, Docs and Sheets through Google's `gws`; only PA can create or change anything, and it asks you each time. Each bot's Google access is set in its settings (none, look up, or change). Setup is in DEVELOPMENT.md.
- **A team wiki as the bots' memory (M9.6):** your bots keep a shared, Karpathy-style knowledge wiki: they look things up there before researching, add what they learn, and every discussion's verdict is filed into it. It runs on the bots' own models, with no extra memory server or GPU.
- **The Orchestrator (M9.5):** a new main bot that plans bigger jobs and coordinates the team: it hands parts to the right bots, starts a discussion when a decision needs several views, runs fleets, and brings it all back as one answer. It's first in the list and opens by default.
- **Discuss (M9.2):** ask a few bots to argue a question out. Each thinks on its own model and sees the others only as a short summary, so its own work stays clean; you watch the discussion live with each bot's face, and the chair writes a verdict with the strongest dissent kept, then files the discussion into memory so any bot can recall it later.
- **Bots can use Orphus's memory (M9.1):** a bot can ask the long-term memory what was decided before, without asking you each time; filing things into memory still asks, and only the librarian may write. Rooms, where bots talk outside their context windows, were already available.
- **Set up fleets in the app (M7.6):** Settings → Fleets: create and edit Orphus fleets (teams of agents, each seat on its own model, that argue a question in a room or split up the work), starting from a council template, and run one as any bot with ▶. Every machine gets them automatically.
- **Every bot has its own face (M7.5):** Researcher has glasses, Coder `< >` brackets, Checker a tick, Blogger a beret, PA a bow tie and Inbox an envelope, each in its own colour; new bots get the classic blue one. The Omarchy bar panel shows the animated faces too.
- **Settings in the app (M7.5):** the gear next to OrphusBot opens Settings: change any bot's model, instructions, thinking and team setting, add bots, choose which bots get each connection and sign in to it, add or remove machines, see usage, link another device, pair a wearable, and sign out.
- **Bots have faces (M7.4):** each bot has a small animated face in the app that shows what it is doing: resting, thinking, working, turning red when it needs you, and smiling when it is done.
- **Better-looking replies (M7.3):** bots now write in one clear house style (answer first, short paragraphs, lists, code blocks, tables where they help). The app shows them with proper headings, highlighted code with a copy button, and tidy tables; the bar panel and notifications show clean text without Markdown symbols.
- **Send your bots pictures and files (M7.2):** in the app, attach with 📎 or paste a picture with Ctrl+V. The bot gets the file in its computer (`inbox/`), and models that can see pictures see it too. Copy any message, save a reply as a file, and save the files bots share.
- **The OrphusBot app (M7.1):** a real desktop app, built with Flutter, for Omarchy now and the Mac and phones next. Bots on the left; on the right the whole conversation with formatted replies, the pictures and files your bots share, approvals and questions answered in place, live status and a Stop button; type at the bottom. Open it from the launcher or the bar panel's **Open chat**.
- **OrphusBot on Omarchy (M8.3):** a bar widget that shows when your bots are working and turns urgent when one needs you, a panel to answer approvals and message any bot, notifications, and `SUPER+ALT+B` to open it. It runs on the new `orphusbot` command (`link`, `status`, `watch`, `answer`, `send`), which works on its own too. Setup is in `omarchy/kelvincushman.orphusbot/README.md`.
- **Bots work as a team (M8.2):** a bot can hand a teammate part of the job ("ask Coder to write the benchmark"); it becomes a ticket for that bot, and the answer comes back to the asking bot, which carries on. Each bot knows who is on the team, and team bots share a `/shared` folder.
- **A model drop-down when setting up a bot:** pick from every model your machines can use, or type one under Other.
- **Laptops work unplugged above 30% battery** (`ORPHUSBOT_MIN_BATTERY` on the node).
- **Pick a bot's model from a list (M8.1):** the Model field suggests every model your machines can use (Claude through Claude Code, Codex, local models such as Qwen on the GPU box), and says which machine has each.
- **A setup script for the Mac:** `sh scripts/macos-setup.sh <user>@<gpu-box>` builds and installs the app and keeps the tunnel to your server open.
- **Sign in to MCP servers (M6.5):** a connection with an address has **Sign in**: you sign in on the provider's page and come back connected. OrphusBot keeps the sign-in fresh before each run, and the tokens stay on the server.
- **MCP servers that run as a command (M6.4):** a connection can be a command such as `npx -y @modelcontextprotocol/server-github`, with the variable its token goes in (`GITHUB_TOKEN`). It runs on your machine only if that node lists the program in `ORPHUSBOT_MCP_COMMANDS`.
- **Capture profiles (M4.7):** Captures → Profile. **Focus support** (the default) keeps everything, sends digests at 10:00, 14:00 and 18:00 plus a 21:00 review, files ideas and things to remember by itself (Undo for a day), brings a dismissed commitment back once after three days, and nudges you 30 and 10 minutes before an appointment with a time and when it starts, by push and on your wrist. **Standard** keeps commitments and appointments with one digest at 18:00. Nothing is ever called overdue: it's "still open".
- **Screens for computers in E2B (M4.6):** watch and take over a bot's E2B computer from the Screen button, like a local one, once the desktop image is built as an E2B template (docs/DEVELOPMENT.md).
- **Fleets (M6.3):** start a message or a routine with `/fleet <name> <task>` (for example `/fleet research-team compare e-bike batteries`) and an Orphus fleet does the work. Every helper it starts asks you first, and every helper's actions are checked and run in the bot's computer, just like the bot's own. Orphus workflows stay switched off.
- **Computers in E2B (M4.5):** tick "In E2B" on a bot and its computer is a cloud sandbox instead of a container on your machines. Set `ORPHUSBOT_E2B_API_KEY` on the server. The bot's thinking still happens on your machine with your subscriptions; only its commands and files go to E2B. The sandbox pauses itself when idle and picks up where it left off.
- **Files from your bots (M6.2):** a bot can hand you a file it made (a report, a spreadsheet, an image, up to 10 MB). It shows in the chat as a download link; sharing the same name again adds a new version.
- **Connections (M6.1):** Settings → **Connections** adds an MCP server (name, address and an optional token) and picks which bots may use it. A bot searches its tools on its own; every call that does something asks you first, by name (for example "Allow Inbox to use mcp:pa_mail_list?"), and **Always allow** covers only that one tool. Tokens are encrypted on the server (set `ORPHUSBOT_SECRET_KEY_FILE`) and hidden as `[secret]` anywhere they would show up.
- **Calibrate System 1 (M5.2):** Decisions → **Fit from labels** works out how sure System 1 should be before it picks a bot or lets a tool call through, from your own approvals and corrections, and shows how the new setting would have done on your newest labels next to the current one. **Apply** starts using it; **Roll back** returns to the one before (or the defaults). A tool call still never passes below 90% sure.
- **System 1 learns from more of what you do (M5.1):** changing a ticket's urgency, and starting or finishing a ticket with the bot System 1 picked, are now kept as labels next to approving cards and changing bots. The Decisions view counts them.
- **Android app (M4.4):** the desktop app now also builds for Android. It's the same window onto your server: enter the server's address once, then sign in with **Sign in with another device** and approve the code from a browser where you have a passkey. Approvals, chat and everything else work as on the web. Build steps are in docs/DEVELOPMENT.md.
- **Ambient capture (M4.3):** turn on hands-free for a wearable (Captures, or the phone app) and what's said around you becomes suggestions: things you said you'd do, things asked of you, and what others owe you, each with the words it came from. Nothing becomes a task until you accept it, and nothing heard ever makes a bot reply or act. Every screen shows when listening is on, with a Pause button; the server can switch it off for everyone (`ORPHUSBOT_HANDS_FREE_ALLOWED=false`). No audio is kept, and the words are deleted after 7 days. Extraction is one call with no tools at all.
- **Usage (M4.2):** Settings → Usage shows what your bots' model calls used today and this week, by provider and model, and today by bot: turns, tokens and what it would have cost. Set `ORPHUSBOT_DAILY_TOKEN_BUDGET` and you get one warning on your wrist and phone when a day's use crosses it.
- **Push to your phone (M4.1):** set `ORPHUSBOT_NTFY_URL` to a topic on your own ntfy server and your phone gets a push when a bot needs you (an approval, a question, a takeover, or an ask your wrist didn't answer), when a task fails, and when work you didn't start from a chat finishes. Tapping one opens that bot. No Google account needed.
- **Decisions view (M3.6):** **Decisions** in the sidebar shows what System 1 decided and how it's doing: how often it was asked, abstained and agreed with you, and every receipt with its confidence, the threshold it needed and how long it took. Approving or denying a card it asked about, and changing the bot on a ticket it assigned, are kept as labels for calibrating it later.
- **The wearable talks to OrphusBot (M3.5):** pair it from Settings → Wearable (a one-time code for the app's pairing page). Tap, speak a task and tap again: it becomes a ticket, and the wrist asks "should I start?" unless System 1 is sure it's safe (tap starts it, double tap leaves it on the board). While it runs the wrist shows queued, running (breathing blue) and done (green flash, double buzz) or error. A bot's question or approval on a task you started from the wrist comes to the wrist one at a time; a tap can approve only a reversible file change you just started, and anything else is "seen" on the wrist and waits on your phone or desktop. Unanswered asks stop after 10 minutes. Settings shows every signal and how it was answered.
- **Tickets (M3.4):** a board (**Tickets** in the sidebar) with To do, Doing, Review, Blocked and Done, urgency and an assigned bot. Assigning a bot starts it on the ticket; when it finishes the ticket moves to Review (or Blocked if it failed). With System 1 on, new tickets get a bot and urgency filled in, and are started only when it's very sure that's safe. Mention `OB-12` in a message and the bot gets that ticket as data.
- **Routines (M3.3):** give a bot something to do on its own, from **Routines** in the sidebar: on a schedule (presets or cron, in your time zone), once at a set time, or whenever its webhook is called (the address is shown once; what's posted to it is given to the bot as data, not instructions). Each firing shows in the bot's thread. Routines skip, and say why, when the bot is disabled or busy, when they've used today's 20 runs, or (if you turn on pre-flight) when System 1 is sure there's nothing new. **Run now** fires one by hand.
- **System 1 with Laya (M3.2):** with laya-serve configured (`ORPHUSBOT_LAYA_URL`), messages to the Inbox go to the right bot when Laya is sure ("Sent to Researcher (System 1 was 92% sure)"), and file changes inside a bot's own computer can go ahead without a card when it's very sure. Shell commands and anything on your machine still always ask. Every decision is recorded and shown under **Decisions**. Without Laya nothing changes.
- **Bots (M3.1):** make as many bots as you like with **+ New bot**: a name, what it's for, instructions, an optional model and thinking level, and a private or team computer. Each bot has its own conversation, its own Orphus engine started with its settings, and its own computer. The **Inbox** bot always exists. Edit or disable a bot from the chat header; the sidebar shows each bot's latest message and what's waiting for you.
- **Several machines and placement (M2.3):** any number of nodes can be connected at once. A bot's work goes back to the machine holding its computer; otherwise to the least busy machine that may work. A laptop on battery, or a node in its quiet hours (`ORPHUSBOT_QUIET_HOURS=22-7`), takes no new work, and a waiting task says why. The chat header shows which machines are online and where the bot is working.
- **Bot screens and takeover (M2.2):** with the new desktop image (`images/desktop`), a bot's computer has a screen with Chromium. Press **Screen** in the chat to watch it live, and **Take control** to use it yourself. A bot can ask you to take over (for a login or a captcha); a card appears with **Open screen** and **Done**, and the bot carries on when you're finished. Screens go through the server and the node's existing link, so the computer opens no ports.
- **Bot computers (M2.1):** a bot's tools (read, write, edit, bash) now run in its own rootless Podman container, not on your machine. The container has no capabilities, a read-only system, its own home folder, resource limits, and internet access but no access to your LAN or NetBird addresses. Your model logins stay on the host. Set `ORPHUSBOT_RUNTIME=none` to keep the old behaviour.
- **Desktop app (M1.5):** `orphusbot-desktop`, a Tauri window onto your server's web UI. It stays on your server and opens other links in your browser; the first run asks for the server's address.
- **Sign in with another device (M1.5):** for the desktop app and any browser without passkeys. The device shows a code such as `ABCD-EFGH`; approve it from a signed-in browser (Settings → Link a device, or the link it shows) and the device gets its own sign-in, which ends if you remove the approving passkey.
- **New web UI (M1.4):** a Leptos app replaces the placeholder page. Bots are on the left (with a badge when one is waiting for you), the chat is on the right with approval and question cards and a Stop button, and Settings lists your passkeys and nodes so you can add or remove them and sign out. It works at phone width and in dark mode. Build it with `trunk build --release` in `crates/ui`; the server serves it from `ORPHUSBOT_UI_DIR`.
- **Node enrolment (M1.3):** each machine gets its own key instead of sharing a token. Choose **Add a node** on the page and run the command it shows (`orphusbot-node enrol <code>`) on the machine; the code works once, for 10 minutes. The node signs a fresh challenge every time it connects. Removing a node (`POST /api/nodes/remove`) disconnects it at once and keeps it out.
- **Passkey sign-in (M1.3):** sign in with a passkey instead of a shared token. On first run, the setup token signs you in for an hour so you can add a passkey; after that it stops working. Sessions last 30 days and only their hashes are stored. Removing a passkey signs out its sessions, and removing all of them turns the setup token back on. New guide for running the server over NetBird with HTTPS: [docs/DEPLOY.md](docs/DEPLOY.md). Nodes can connect over `wss://`.
- **Approvals and the effect ledger (M1.2):** every tool call the bot makes goes to OrphusBot first through the `orphusbot-bridge` Orphus extension, which the node loads automatically. Read-only tools run; anything else shows an approval card (Allow once, Always allow this tool, Deny) unless a rule already decides. A resumed run can't repeat a call that already completed: it gets the earlier result instead, and a call that may have run before it was interrupted is flagged for you. Questions a bot asks appear as cards you can answer.
- **Durable runs (M1.1):** each message becomes a run in Postgres. If the node drops or goes silent mid-run, the run goes back to the queue and resumes, with its context, when a node is available again; a late report from the interrupted attempt is ignored. A message sent while the bot is waiting joins its run, and one sent while it is working steers it. A **Stop** button cancels the run.
- **Milestone M0:** a Rust workspace with `orphusbot-proto`, `orphusbot-server` and `orphusbot-node`. A message sent from the browser is stored in Postgres, handed to an `orphus --mode rpc` engine on a node, and the reply streams back to every open browser over Server-Sent Events, resuming from the last event after a reconnect. See [docs/DEVELOPMENT.md](docs/DEVELOPMENT.md).
- Rust CI: formatting, Clippy with warnings as errors, tests against Postgres 18, and `cargo deny` for licences and advisories.
- The wearable (firmware, Flutter companion app, hardware, device-channel contract and docs) in `wearable/`, imported as a cleaned snapshot ([DECISIONS D-012](docs/DECISIONS.md)).
- `.gitleaksignore` listing the reviewed secret-scan false positives.
- Product requirements ([docs/PRD.md](docs/PRD.md)), design ([docs/DESIGN.md](docs/DESIGN.md)), decision log ([docs/DECISIONS.md](docs/DECISIONS.md)) and the Rakazo architecture study.
- Contribution rules: documentation-in-the-same-PR, the ponytail method and Rust practices ([CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md), [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md)).
- CodeRabbit review configuration and a CI check that pull requests update docs and the changelog.

### Fixed

- Android: pictures can be added to the gallery. **Add files** now shows when choosing from the gallery for a message too (new files are selected), and uploads read Android's picked files directly.
- **Bots know today's date and time:** every task now starts with the date and time in your time zone, so a bot no longer guesses the day (PA had called a Tuesday "Friday").
- **Bots no longer think they have Google before it's set up:** they were told about their Google access even though Google wasn't connected yet, so a calendar request bounced between bots. Until Google is set up they now say so plainly.
- **Changing a bot's model works, and you can do it from the chat:** the model list in Settings was empty in the app, so only the default could be chosen. Choosing a model now opens a searchable list of every model your machines have (or type your own), and the chat header shows the bot's model; click it to switch.
- **Messages are never lost when the server is unreachable:** the app waits and retries for up to two minutes (for example while the tunnel reconnects after the server restarts), says so in plain words, and if it still can't send, puts your text and files back in the box.
- **Fleets no longer stall:** a bot that starts helpers now waits for them, instead of finishing while they sit waiting for approvals nobody could give, and each fleet run starts with a clean slate, separate from the bot's own chat.
- The Omarchy panel now shows the chosen bot's reply (and "working…" while it thinks), and the "finished" notification carries the reply; before, a message sent from the panel was answered but you never saw the answer.
- Bots without a computer could start Orphus helpers (`subagent`, `workflow`, `fleet`) once you approved that call, and the helpers then ran commands without asking. Those tools are now refused everywhere.
- Choice lists (a bot's thinking level, a ticket's bot, urgency and status, a routine's bot and schedule) showed their first option instead of the saved one, so editing a bot could reset its thinking level. They now show the saved value.
- The ticket board now refreshes while it's open, so a bot System 1 assigns to a new ticket appears without reopening it.
